Spiral Design - The spiral design process starts out with identifying the objective, which I did. The identified problem for my project is the jumbled mess of Christmas decorations and non-reusable supplies. The second step, the risk analysis, is very low for this project since it is an attempt at fixing an issue, but if it is not successful there is a very low startup cost and a small amount of material waste. The third step, the engineering, is to be done by me later on by understanding how I can create the individual pieces to bring a whole assembly together. Lastly is the evaluation, this step is also to be done later once I have a prototype piece to evaluate. 

FDM anisotropic - The anisotropic effects of FDM printing are the "wood grain" effects that come with 3D printing. There is a stronger and weaker way to lay the grain so that the parts will be stronger in certain parts or weaker in others. This effect will not have to be taken into account much for my project as it will undergo a minimal amount of force throughout its' lifespan. For other projects, this effect matters a lot and can be adjusted in the slicing step of printing when the builder is ready to 3D print the objects. 

SLA Shrinkage - I will not be using SLA printing in my project so the shrinkage would not affect me very much, however if I had smaller parts that required tight fits, that would cause me to have to account for the small amount of shrinkage in the curing part of the SLA printing process. 

Laser Cut Kerf - For my project, the kerf will not be a major issue as the laser cut part of my project will not need to be a tight fit like our previous projects. That being said, I have previously calculated kerf based on the same type and thickness of wood, and I will take that into account when making my parts.
