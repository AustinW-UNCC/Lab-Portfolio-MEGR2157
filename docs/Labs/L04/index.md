# A4 – [Benchmark a Parameter]

Parameter - 

I decided to design an overhang test in order to see how well the Prusa Core One would handle overhangs above 45 degrees. I expect the machine to handle up to the 47 degree overhang with minimal flaw in the print. However, once it gets to the 49 degree overhang, I expect a possibly full failure or heavy amounts of "String" in the print.

This print tested the ability of the Prusa Core One to handles overhangs in prints. I started with 43 degrees and then went up by 2 degrees each piece in order to narrow down exactly when the printer would start to fail. The printer says that it can handle up to 45 degrees without supports but I was curious if I would start to see points of failure prior to or even atv 45 degrees. If I didn't, then the test still allowed for me to see those failure points at 47 and 49 degrees respectively.

Lessons Learned - 

  - the outcome was different than I originally expected and I am pretty upset with myself for not test much higher variations of overhangs.

  - the first thing I would change is to start at the 45 degree mark because there seems to be no need for the 43 degree part at this point.

  - next, I would try to go in intervals of 5 degrees instead of 2 degrees because I clearly didn't go high enough to make the printer fail.

  - I would also change the length of the 4 towers because I believe that the longer they are, the more likely they are to fail.

  - lastly, I learned that the printers themselves can handle more than they advertise, this is probably done since most people who own a printer like that aren't making super detailed and important parts so they will be much more likely to not care about exact angles on overhangs.

  - this took about 3.5 hours from the total design time to the final print and the completion of this lab report.
