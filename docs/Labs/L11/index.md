# A11 – Snap Fit Resin Print

Objective - Design and 3D print a small artifact composed of two or more parts that snap together using the Form 4 SLA printer.
The goal is to design, test, and document a functional snap fit while understanding how material properties, tolerances, and printer settings affect assembly performance and durability.

You may design any small artifact, provided that:

It demonstrates a working snap fit between two or more components.

It is not just a plain test block; the design must be creative and/or useful (e.g., a small enclosure, clip, latch, hinge, or functional connector).

2–3 student projects will share one build plate.

Research and Principles - My design is a cantilever-based lever. The main principle that I decided to pay attention to on this build is how the snap fits can be used to hold things together other than just attaching to themselves. 

There were no sketches or drawings as I just started to make this part in CAD without a pretensive drawing. 

The mechanical properties that govern performance including stress, strain, and stiffness.

The curing process took about another 30 minutes because the machine had to heat up and get ready, however, the actual curing process only took about 10 minutes. This step was very easy and the one mistake that I made was curing it before I had the chance to try and break the two parts apart. Had I done that before curing, it may have been much easier. The surface finish was nice and I was a big fan of the clear as it is very visually appealing. 

Validation - 

The snap fit part of my design works perfectly and had zero issues coming together. The only part that did not work was the slide fit between the two pieces that holds them together as one. I had to scale down my design because of the constraints on how much material we could use, and that caused the printer to make the tolerances on the slide fit too tight. However, I broke them apart and the snap fit part works as intended still.

Lessons Learned - 

The biggest thing that I learned through this process was that scaling down parts seems to affect tolerances more than it does when 3D printing. I should have given myself enough time to run a test print prior to doing this, or atleast giving myself more time to print a full size part as that would have still likely worked out just fine. The snap fits seem to have a stronger grip when using SLA and part of that could be to me having more experience using them, while it also could be due to the increased accuracy in the build quality. This whole project took me between 4-5 hours to complete including this documentation. I only used about half of the allowed SLA fluid as alloted for us in the class.
