# L2 – Design and Slicing

One design rule that is specific to additive manufacturing is to design products such that they can be oriented with as little use of supports are possible. This is extremely important for mass manufacturing as it greatly reduces time spent printing and allows for more products to be made in less time. If designers were to disregard how the product needs to be oriented while printing, the entire process of 3D printing would not be able to keep up with the demand of manufacturing. 


FDM Consideration - 

One specific consideration in regard to FDM is overhangs. Overhangs are sections of the piece that you are making that have no direct contact to the rest of the part and are "hanging" over thing air. Now, if an overhang is small and at angles less than 45 degrees, you can get away with them by slowly building up to them using a normal 3D printer. However, if they are more extreme overhangs, then the designer must either expect to add supports to the print or find a way to incorporate connecting pieces into the design to reduce the overhangs on the product.  

Something I learned from a teammate - 

Infill decisions are affected more by the need to reduce time and waste rather than based solely on how strong the component needs to be. People would rather focus on reduction of costs and material than to over-engineer a product that may not be very expensive or profitable.

After opening Prusa Slicer I dropped the scale of the toy to 70% so that it fit within the requirements of time and size that we were allowed/required to print. There was no need for supports in this case because I have zero overhangs or empty spaces inside of the print that would require them. Aside from this, there was nothing extra that was needed to ensure proper printing as this is a very basic and simple item/design, and it allowed me to become more familiar with a new slicing software without having to worry about making too many changes.

Lessons Learned: 

  - The Prusa printers are capable of handling a pretty tight tolerance item and there are no signs of sticking or overlapping in the different layers of the print.
  - One thing of note that a lot of my classmates made a mistake on, was that they were sending the G-code instead of the .stl file to other classmates when trying to put them all together for printing. One of my group members did this and luckily, I caught it and was even able to help out a few other groups who had the same issue.
  - I learned that PETG is much more flexible than I imagined it to be while also feeling a lot like PLA in its hardness. I would definitely use PETG in the future for anything that I feel may go through some stress or strain as PLA will break very easily.
  - If there was one thing I would've changed throughout the process, it would have been to have 3 group members instead of 4 total because one of my classmates had to stay behind and wait for the prints to finish and bring mine to me the next day since I had to leave for another class as soon as ours ended.
